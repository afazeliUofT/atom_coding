\section{Authoritative verdict on the v3 evidence}
The v3 computation established a coherent theory-and-algorithm core:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item exact complete-tie agreement on exhaustive micro-worlds;
\item a codebook-free membership-only decoder using syndromes or checksums;
\item a strict unseen-candidate certificate;
\item a deterministic fixed-edit shell upper bound;
\item history-revelation moment and tail exponents inherited from BSC guesswork;
\item a typical-versus-mean phase diagram;
\item finite-length wins over exact prefix and syndrome-trellis searches in low-substitution regimes.
\end{enumerate}
The same evidence also revealed hard boundaries.  At rate $R=0.75$, the theoretical mean-work boundary is near $p=0.009$, much lower than the fixed-quantile boundary near $p=0.0417$.  The v3 $p=0.04$ rows displayed severe search tails, and the generic decoder was much slower than a true linear-time VT specialization.  The programme is therefore not a general insertion/deletion decoder; it is a fixed-edit, low-substitution, high-rate candidate.

The v3 computational decision was intentionally permissive.  Three observations prevent immediate manuscript freeze:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item the largest length was $n=40$ with only three trials per row;
\item percentile labels from three observations are not statistically meaningful tail estimates;
\item Python implementations can favor one search organization because of array allocation, interpreter, and dynamic-dispatch costs.
\end{enumerate}
Version 4 addresses these deficiencies before manuscript investment.

\section{Permanent novelty boundary}
The following claims are prohibited.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Fiber guesswork is a new random variable.}  Ranking $X$ by $W(Y\mid X)$ under a uniform prior is ordinary conditional guesswork \cite{Arikan1996,Li2018}.
\item \emph{Best path versus best aggregate candidate is new.}  The distinction is established in probabilistic automata and weighted inference \cite{HigueraOncina2014,Mohri2009}.
\item \emph{Priority queues, A$^\star$, or path summation are new.}  Priority-first ML decoding and synchronization sequential decoding are classical lines \cite{Gallager1961,Han1993,Banerjee2022}.
\item \emph{General channel candidate guessing is new.}  Guessing-based decoding for general DMCs and unified GRAND/GCD formulations already exist \cite{TanJoudeh2025,Wang2025}.
\item \emph{Efficient exact decoding holds for unrestricted weighted transducers.}  Best-string inference can be hard, and the current algorithm is XP rather than FPT in edit count \cite{HigueraOncina2014,Fijalkow2022}.
\end{enumerate}

The narrow residual claim is:
\begin{quote}
For a fixed number of uniformly located deletions followed by survivor BSC substitutions, a codebook-free membership-only aggregate inverse decoder admits a strict exact-ML certificate.  Its distinct-candidate query count is exponent-optimal within an explicit L0 membership-oracle model, its history work obeys fixed-edit moment and tail laws, and a hybrid with code-side exact search has distinct typical and mean operating boundaries.
\end{quote}
No located primary source contains this complete theorem-and-algorithm package.  Several ingredients are known separately, including fixed-deletion multiplicity-aware likelihoods \cite{Sabary2022}, edit-frame enumeration in an earlier patent \cite{US4922494}, and specialized deletion/substitution code constructions \cite{Song2026,PiZhang2025}.  The correct novelty label is \emph{distinct but narrow}, subject to independent external adjudication.

\section{Channel model and exact membership-first decoder}
Let $x\in\{0,1\}^n$.  Exactly $t<n$ positions are selected uniformly without replacement and deleted.  The $m=n-t$ survivors pass through a BSC with crossover probability $0\le p<1/2$, producing $y\in\{0,1\}^m$.  Let
\[
\J_t=\{J\subseteq[n]:|J|=t\},\qquad S=|\J_t|=\binom nt.
\]
For $J\in\J_t$, let $d_J(x,y)$ be the Hamming distance between $y$ and the subsequence of $x$ obtained by deleting $J$.  The exact likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_y(x)=\frac1S\sum_{J\in\J_t}p^{d_J(x,y)}(1-p)^{m-d_J(x,y)}.
\label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}

An inverse history is $h=(J,b,e)$, where $b\in\{0,1\}^t$ fills the deleted symbols and $e\in\{0,1\}^m$ is the survivor substitution pattern.  It creates
\[
x_h=\operatorname{Ins}_J(y\oplus e,b)
\]
with component weight
\[
\omega(h)=S^{-1}p^{\operatorname{wt}(e)}(1-p)^{m-\operatorname{wt}(e)}.
\]
For each $J$ and complete $x$, there is exactly one compatible $(b,e)$.

The decoder maintains one probability-ordered stream for every $J$.  At first discovery of a distinct $x$, it queries \texttt{IsCodeword}$(x)$.  It evaluates the complete sum \eqref{eq:likelihood} only for codewords.  If $\eta_J$ is the next unprocessed component weight in stream $J$, then every completely unseen candidate satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_y(x)\le U_{\rm new}:=\sum_{J\in\J_t}\eta_J.
\label{eq:unseen}
\end{equation}
If the largest exactly evaluated codeword score $s^\star$ satisfies $s^\star>U_{\rm new}$, the complete discovered tie set at $s^\star$ is the global ML tie set.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Membership-first certified FIBER search}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $y,t,p$, membership oracle for $\C$
\State initialize one probability-ordered inverse stream for every $J\in\J_t$
\State $D\gets\emptyset$, $s^\star\gets-\infty$, $T^\star\gets\emptyset$
\While{some stream remains}
  \State pop the globally largest next history and advance its stream
  \State construct $x_h$
  \If{$x_h\notin D$}
    \State $D\gets D\cup\{x_h\}$ and query \texttt{IsCodeword}$(x_h)$
    \If{$x_h\in\C$}
      \State compute exact $\Lambda_y(x_h)$ and update $(s^\star,T^\star)$
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \State $U_{\rm new}\gets\sum_J\eta_J$
  \If{$T^\star\ne\emptyset$ and $s^\star>U_{\rm new}$}
    \State \Return $T^\star$ with strict ML certificate
  \EndIf
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{New candidate-volume and membership-oracle theory}
This section gives the main new contribution of version 4.  Unlike the history-revelation theorem, it directly compares the realized decoder with the best possible exact decoder under the same weak code interface.

\subsection{Candidate shells}
Define the best-alignment mismatch
\[
\delta_y(x):=\min_{J\in\J_t}d_J(x,y)
\]
and the inverse candidate-shell volume
\[
V_y(w):=\left|\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:\delta_y(x)\le w\}\right|.
\]
Let
\[
B_m(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{w,m\}}\binom mi,
\]
with $B_m(w)=0$ for $w<0$.

\begin{proposition}[Candidate-volume and ambiguity bounds]
\label{prop:volume}
For every observation $y$ and integer $w$,
\begin{equation}
2^tB_m(w)\le V_y(w)\le 2^t\binom nt B_m(w).
\label{eq:volume}
\end{equation}
Moreover, every complete candidate $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ has exactly $\binom nt$ compatible inverse histories.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix one deletion set $J$.  The map $(e,b)\mapsto\operatorname{Ins}_J(y\oplus e,b)$ is injective, so the histories with $\operatorname{wt}(e)\le w$ create exactly $2^tB_m(w)$ distinct candidates.  This proves the lower bound.  Taking the union over the $S=\binom nt$ deletion sets proves the upper bound.  For fixed $(x,J)$, the deleted bits $b$ are the symbols of $x$ in $J$, and $e=y\oplus\operatorname{Del}_J(x)$ is unique.  Hence there is one compatible history per $J$.
\end{proof}

Define the largest individual component supporting $x$:
\[
M_y(x)=\frac1S p^{\delta_y(x)}(1-p)^{m-\delta_y(x)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
M_y(x)\le\Lambda_y(x)\le S M_y(x).
\label{eq:sandwich}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The L0 oracle model}
\begin{definition}[L0 membership-oracle decoder]
An L0 decoder knows the channel and observation and may perform arbitrary channel-side computation, but it learns the code only through adaptive queries of the form \texttt{IsCodeword}$(x)$.  It must return an exact ML decision for every membership-testable codebook $\C\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Mandatory higher-likelihood queries]
\label{lem:mandatory}
Before certifying a codeword $c^\star$, every exact L0 decoder must query every candidate $x$ satisfying
\[
\Lambda_y(x)>\Lambda_y(c^\star).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If such an $x$ remains unqueried, the transcript is consistent with another membership oracle that agrees on every previous query but also declares $x$ a codeword.  Under that oracle, $c^\star$ is not ML.  Thus the original transcript cannot certify an exact answer for every codebook.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Query-competitiveness theorem}
Let $c^\star$ be an ML codeword and write $d^\star=\delta_y(c^\star)$.  For $p>0$, set
\[
a=\frac{1-p}{p},\qquad L_{n,t}=\left\lfloor\log_a\binom nt\right\rfloor.
\]
Let $\Qopt$ be the minimum number of distinct membership queries made by any exact L0 decoder on this instance, and let $\Qfib$ be the number made by FIBER.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle-relative candidate-query sandwich]
\label{thm:oracle}
For $0<p<1/2$,
\begin{equation}
V_y(d^\star-L_{n,t}-1)
\le \Qopt
\le \Qfib
\le V_y(d^\star+L_{n,t}).
\label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
At $p=0$, the same argument gives a polynomial fixed-edit bound after shell zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\delta_y(x)\le d^\star-L_{n,t}-1$, then by \eqref{eq:sandwich},
\[
\Lambda_y(x)\ge M_y(x)
\ge \frac1S p^{d^\star-L_{n,t}-1}(1-p)^{m-d^\star+L_{n,t}+1}
>p^{d^\star}(1-p)^{m-d^\star}
\ge\Lambda_y(c^\star),
\]
because $a^{L_{n,t}+1}>S$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:mandatory} proves the first inequality.  The middle inequality is the definition of an optimal L0 query count.  FIBER discovers $c^\star$ by shell $d^\star$.  After all histories through shell $d^\star+L_{n,t}$ have been emitted, the sum-of-frontiers bound is strictly below the probability of one best supporting history of $c^\star$, and therefore below $\Lambda_y(c^\star)$.  The certificate has fired, and every queried candidate lies in $V_y(d^\star+L_{n,t})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Subexponential and interior-shell polynomial competitiveness]
\label{cor:competitive}
For fixed $t$, if $d^\star/m\to q\in(0,1/2)$, then
\[
\frac1n\log_2\Qopt
=\frac1n\log_2\Qfib+o(1)
=\hbin(q)+o(1),
\]
and
\[
\frac{\Qfib}{\Qopt}\le n^{O(1)}.
\]
Thus FIBER is candidate-query exponent-optimal in the L0 oracle model on every such instance sequence.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $t$, $L_{n,t}=O(\log n)$ and $S=n^{O(1)}$.  Standard Hamming-ball estimates applied to \eqref{eq:volume} give
\[
\log_2 B_m(d^\star\pm O(\log n))=m\hbin(q)+O(\log n).
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope]
The lower bound is an oracle result, not a processor-cycle converse.  A decoder exploiting a parity-check matrix, trellis, or algebraic completion oracle is outside L0 and can be faster.  This is why the experimental gate compares FIBER with code-aware exact searches and retains the VT specialization boundary.
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed-edit history moments, tails, and phase boundaries}
Let $\Rhist$ be the probability-order rank of the actual inverse history.  With
\[
K_n=2^t\binom nt,\qquad B_m(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{k,m\}}\binom mi,
\]
and actual survivor error weight $E=e$,
\[
1+K_nB_m(e-1)\le\Rhist\le K_nB_m(e).
\]
For fixed $t$, $0<p<1/2$, and $\rho>0$,
\begin{equation}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log_2\mathbb E[\Rhist^\rho]
=\rho H_{1/(1+\rho)}(p),
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
and the actual certified FIBER work obeys the corresponding upper moment bound.  Equality in \eqref{eq:moment} concerns history revelation, not every code-dependent stopping time.

For $\gamma\in[\hbin(p),1)$, let $q_\gamma\in[p,1/2)$ solve $\hbin(q_\gamma)=\gamma$.  Then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac1n\log_2\Pr\left\{n^{-1}\log_2\Rhist\ge\gamma\right\}
=\Dkl(q_\gamma\Vert p).
\]
Every fixed quantile has exponent $\hbin(p)$, while the mean exponent is $H_{1/2}(p)$.

If an independent code-side exact decoder uses at most $\poly(n)2^{n(1-R)}$ work, a parallel hybrid has upper-envelope boundaries
\[
\min\{\hbin(p),1-R\}
\quad\text{for fixed quantiles},
\]
and
\[
\min\{H_{1/2}(p),1-R\}
\quad\text{for mean work}.
\]
The resulting thresholds are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cccc@{}}
\toprule
$R$ & $1-R$ & $p_{\rm typ}$: $\hbin(p)=1-R$ & $p_{\rm mean}$: $H_{1/2}(p)=1-R$\\
\midrule
0.625 & 0.375 & 0.07245 & 0.02254\\
0.750 & 0.250 & 0.04169 & 0.00903\\
0.875 & 0.125 & 0.01713 & 0.00205\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are exponent boundaries, not finite-length guarantees.

\section{Same-toolchain compiled flagship benchmark}
The version-4 benchmark compiles both FIBER and its exact code-aware references in one C++20 translation unit using the same compiler, optimization flags, channel likelihood, code representation, and timing clock.  Decoder execution order is randomized for each block.  The benchmark includes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a membership-only FIBER history search;
\item a systematic-code prefix A$^\star$ search with an admissible aggregate likelihood bound;
\item a syndrome-trellis aggregate A$^\star$ reference when its dynamic-programming state budget is feasible;
\item exhaustive small-world self-tests against ML.
\end{enumerate}
At the largest lengths, compiled prefix A$^\star$ is the authoritative timed reference.  The syndrome-trellis decoder is timed only where its dynamic-programming state budget is feasible and is independently exercised against exhaustive ML in the compiled self-test.  Random-linear and reproducible CRC-polynomial codes use syndrome membership without materialized codebooks.

The standard profile tests $n\in\{32,48,64\}$, rates $0.75$ and $0.875$, and probabilities concentrated around the theoretically favorable region.  It reports exact agreement, completion, median ratio with bootstrap confidence interval, 90/95/99th observed ratios, raw histories, membership queries, prefix/trellis states, and fitted scaling slopes.  Boundary configurations are retained even when FIBER loses.

The gate is deliberately stricter than v3:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item all exact decoders must certify and agree;
\item at least four largest-length favorable configurations must show a median $\ge1.25\times$ FIBER speedup;
\item at least three must have a bootstrap upper confidence endpoint below parity;
\item at least three must have observed 95th-percentile ratio at most $1.25$;
\item favorable scaling may not systematically degrade;
\item every mean-favorable row must remain within a $1.25$ median ratio.
\end{itemize}
No finite trial count can certify a $99.9$ percentile.  A positive gate only establishes manuscript potential and defines the larger publication campaign.

\section{Specialization and real-system boundaries}
A true linear-time VT decoder is expected to dominate generic FIBER on the noiseless single-deletion VT problem.  This is a positive scientific boundary: FIBER's value is code modularity under noisy ambiguous edits, not replacement of an optimal specialized decoder.

A measured post-front-end trace is optional for a theory-centered IEEE TIT manuscript.  It is mandatory for statements about timing slips in deployed receivers, telecommunications value, or field-defining system impact.  The package accepts measured CSV traces and audits whether they occupy the bounded-edit, low-substitution region; it does not relabel synthetic Monte Carlo data as measurement.

\section{Flagship manuscript contract}
A positive version-4 run authorizes a full manuscript with the following core.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Exact fixed-edit likelihood and membership-first aggregate inverse decoder.
\item Strict complete-tie ML certificate.
\item Candidate-volume and exact ambiguity theorem.
\item L0 membership-oracle lower bound and FIBER query-competitiveness theorem.
\item History moment and large-deviation specialization, with correct equality/upper-bound boundary.
\item Typical-versus-mean phase diagram and hybrid decoder interpretation.
\item Exact micro-world verification and same-toolchain compiled finite-length results.
\item Honest negative regimes, VT specialization boundary, and XP-not-FPT statement.
\end{enumerate}

The paper must not claim universal synchronization decoding, generic fiber-guesswork novelty, FPT edit dependence, or state-of-the-art system superiority.  An external reviewer must independently verify every theorem and the primary-source novelty matrix before submission.

\section{External-review checklist}
The independent review must answer:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Is Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} valid for the stated arbitrary-code L0 oracle model?
\item Does Corollary~\ref{cor:competitive} require any additional uniformity assumptions on Hamming-ball estimates?
\item Is the complete-tie certificate strict and numerically defensible?
\item Does any primary source already contain the complete candidate-volume/oracle-competitiveness result?
\item Are the moment and tail equalities correctly restricted to history revelation?
\item Are the compiled references exact and fairly charged?
\item Are all state-of-the-art and specialization boundaries represented honestly?
\end{enumerate}
A blocking defect in Questions 1--5 is an immediate stop or major narrowing event.

\section{Conclusion}
The v3 evidence justifies continued work but not a flagship verdict by itself.  The strongest surviving path is no longer a broad new decoding field.  It is a precise fixed-edit theorem-and-algorithm package: exact aggregate inverse search through a weak membership interface, an auditable ML certificate, oracle-relative candidate-query optimality, and sharply different typical and mean complexity regions.

If version 4 reproduces the mathematical audits and a robust compiled advantage in the predicted low-substitution regime, a quality IEEE TIT flagship candidate is scientifically justified.  If the compiled gate fails, the theory remains potentially publishable, but the flagship-performance objective must be narrowed.  Real-system and field-defining claims remain conditional on measured receiver evidence and independent adoption.
