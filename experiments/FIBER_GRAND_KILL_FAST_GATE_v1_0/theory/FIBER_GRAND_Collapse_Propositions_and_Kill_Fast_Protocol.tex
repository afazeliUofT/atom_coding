\begin{abstract}
The proposal under audit makes a correct and important observation: on a non-invertible random-transformation channel, maximum-likelihood (ML) decoding ranks candidate inputs by the \emph{sum} of all latent-history probabilities that generate the observation, not by the probability of the first or best individual history. The one-deletion-plus-substitution likelihood and its simple unseen-candidate bound are mathematically sound. The proposal's field-defining case, however, cannot rest merely on defining a transformation fiber, restating posterior candidate rank as ``fiber guesswork,'' or applying a generic branch-and-bound certificate. Conditional guesswork with side information is classical, and admissible best-first/priority-first search, alignment summation, weighted transducers, and synchronization decoding have substantial prior art.

This dossier identifies the narrower scientific residue that could still be foundational: a code-modular aggregate inverse-search engine whose \emph{fully accounted} exact work is substantially below codeword-wise ML; a theory of search inflation and ambiguity overhead; and a tractability boundary for bounded-edit or bounded-drift channels. It proves the exact membership-first form of the one-deletion decoder, generalizes the unseen bound to fixed edit-position stream families, and specifies a reproducible experiment suite with preregistered GO/NARROW/STOP thresholds. The computational package checks exactness exhaustively, measures how often pathwise first-hit is wrong, compares history-driven and prefix-driven certified search against exhaustive codeword ML, tests one insertion and two deletions, and transfers the same channel engine across random-linear, CRC-like, Hamming, and VT codebooks. A positive standard run authorizes only a revised claim contract; it cannot by itself establish novelty, superiority over specialized synchronization decoders, or field-defining status.
\end{abstract}

\section{Proposal reconstruction and scientific boundary}

\subsection{The proposal's central claim}
Let a latent transformation history $Z=z$ act on an input $x$ through $T_z$, producing $Y=T_z(x)$. For fixed $(x,y)$, define the inverse history fiber
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_y(x)=\{z:T_z(x)=y\},
\end{equation}
and its aggregate score
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_y(x)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal F_y(x)}p(z\mid x)=W(y\mid x).
\label{eq:aggregate}
\end{equation}
For a uniform prior over a codebook $\cC$, exact ML decoding is
\begin{equation}
\widehat c_{\rm ML}(y)\in\arg\max_{c\in\cC}\Lambda_y(c).
\end{equation}
The proposal correctly distinguishes this from ranking candidates by
\begin{equation}
M_y(x)=\max_{z\in\mathcal F_y(x)}p(z\mid x),
\end{equation}
which is a best-path rather than best-candidate criterion.

\subsection{What is scientifically correct and immediately useful}
The following statements survive direct audit.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Multiplicity can reverse the candidate order.} For $\cC=\{000,001\}$, observation $00$, and one uniform deletion with no substitutions, $\Lambda_{00}(000)=1$ while $\Lambda_{00}(001)=1/3$, although every compatible individual deletion history has mass $1/3$.
\item \textbf{The fixed-observation order condition is exact.} A pathwise first-hit rule is ML for every codebook if and only if its induced candidate order never places a lower-$\Lambda_y$ candidate before a higher-$\Lambda_y$ candidate.
\item \textbf{The general lazy certificate is valid.} If an incumbent codeword lower bound exceeds every discovered-competitor upper bound and a valid upper bound on every undiscovered candidate, the incumbent is exact ML.
\item \textbf{The one-deletion likelihood is linear-time per candidate.} All deletion-alignment mismatch counts can be updated by a one-step recurrence.
\item \textbf{The simple multi-stream unseen bound is valid.} If $\eta_j$ is the largest unprocessed component in deletion stream $j$, every completely unseen candidate has score at most $\sum_j\eta_j$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What cannot carry a field-defining novelty claim}
Three conceptual corrections are mandatory.

\paragraph{Candidate rank is conditional guesswork.}
Under a uniform prior on $X$, Bayes' rule gives
\begin{equation}
P_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)\propto W(y\mid x)=\Lambda_y(x).
\end{equation}
Therefore the proposal's
\begin{equation}
G_{\rm fib}(X\mid Y)=\operatorname{rank}_{\Lambda_Y}(X)
\end{equation}
is exactly the optimal posterior guessing rank of $X$ given side information $Y$. Guessing with side information and its conditional R\'enyi characterization were studied by Ar\i kan and subsequently sharpened nonasymptotically by Sason and Verd\'u~\cite{arikan1996,sasonverdu2018}. The term ``fiber guesswork'' may remain a channel-specific interpretation, but not a new generic random variable.

\paragraph{The stopping lemma is a branch-and-bound certificate.}
The inequality comparing an incumbent lower bound to frontier or residual upper bounds is mathematically useful, but it is structurally a standard admissible-search certificate. Sequential synchronization decoding, priority-first ML decoding, weighted automata, and best-first search all provide relevant prior foundations~\cite{gallager1961,han1993,mohri2009}. Novelty must lie in a new bound, a new complexity theorem, or a new code-modular implementation advantage---not in the abstract certificate alone.

\paragraph{Summing hidden alignments is established inference semantics.}
Forward summation over deletion/insertion alignments, pair-HMM paths, and weighted-transducer paths is established. ML deletion analyses explicitly depend on embedding/alignment multiplicity~\cite{davey2001,sabary2020,sabary2022}. The proposal's defensible distinction is not ``sum the paths,'' but rather:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{aggregate inverse-channel search} + \text{generic code interface} + \text{runtime exact certificate} + \text{work-tail theory}.}
\end{equation}

\section{Minimal mathematical development}

\subsection{One deletion plus substitutions}
Let $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\{0,1\}^n$. Each bit is independently flipped with probability $p<1/2$, after which exactly one location $J$ is deleted with probability $q_j$. The observation has length $n-1$. For deletion position $j$, define
\begin{equation}
d_j(x,y)=\sum_{i<j}\mathbf 1\{x_i\ne y_i\}+\sum_{i>j}\mathbf 1\{x_i\ne y_{i-1}\}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_y(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_j p^{d_j(x,y)}(1-p)^{n-1-d_j(x,y)}.
\label{eq:d1score}
\end{equation}
All $d_j$ are obtained in $O(n)$ time from
\begin{align}
d_1(x,y)&=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\mathbf 1\{x_i\ne y_{i-1}\},\\
d_{j+1}(x,y)&=d_j(x,y)-\mathbf 1\{x_{j+1}\ne y_j\}+\mathbf 1\{x_j\ne y_j\}.
\end{align}

\subsection{History streams and the unseen-candidate bound}
For each $j$, enumerate surviving-bit substitution patterns $e\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$ in nonincreasing BSC mass, and both possible deleted input bits $b\in\{0,1\}$. The inverse candidate is
\begin{equation}
x=\operatorname{Ins}_j(y\oplus e,b),
\end{equation}
with component mass
\begin{equation}
\omega_j(e)=q_jp^{w_H(e)}(1-p)^{n-1-w_H(e)}.
\end{equation}
Let $\eta_j(t)$ be the next unprocessed mass in stream $j$.

\begin{theorem}[Unseen-candidate bound and exact stopping]
At any search time $t$, every candidate never emitted by any stream satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_y(x)\le U_t^{\rm new}:=\sum_{j=1}^n\eta_j(t).
\end{equation}
Assume every discovered complete candidate has been membership-tested and every discovered codeword has been exactly scored. Let
\begin{equation}
s_t^\star=\max_{c\in\cC\cap D_t}\Lambda_y(c),\qquad
T_t^\star=\{c\in\cC\cap D_t:\Lambda_y(c)=s_t^\star\},
\end{equation}
where $D_t$ is the set of discovered candidates. If $\cC\cap D_t\neq\varnothing$ and
\begin{equation}
s_t^\star>U_t^{\rm new},
\end{equation}
then $T_t^\star$ is exactly the global ML tie set. In particular, the ML codeword is unique if and only if $|T_t^\star|=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an unseen $x$, its unique component in each alignment stream has not been emitted and therefore has mass at most that stream's next mass $\eta_j(t)$. Summing the $n$ alignment contributions proves the bound. Every discovered codeword has its exact score represented in $s_t^\star$ and $T_t^\star$, while discovered noncodewords are outside the optimization domain. The strict inequality excludes every undiscovered codeword from tying or exceeding $s_t^\star$, so the global ML tie set is precisely $T_t^\star$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Membership-first refinement}
The proposal's literal Algorithm~1 exactly scores every newly discovered candidate before checking code membership. That work is unnecessary.

\begin{proposition}[Membership-first exactness]
Assume (i) every newly discovered complete candidate is subjected to an exact code-membership test, (ii) every discovered codeword is exactly scored, and (iii) $U_t^{\rm new}$ remains a valid bound on every completely undiscovered candidate. Then discovered noncodewords need not be exactly scored. Stopping when the best discovered-codeword score exceeds $U_t^{\rm new}$ remains exact ML.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A discovered noncodeword is not in the maximization domain $\cC$. The only possible competitors are discovered codewords, which are exactly scored, and undiscovered codewords, each bounded by $U_t^{\rm new}$.
\end{proof}

This refinement is essential for the code-agnostic claim: with a cheap membership oracle, complete likelihood evaluation is performed only for the small subset of emitted candidates that are codewords.

\subsection{Prefix aggregate search}
For a candidate prefix $u$, maintain alignment-specific mismatch counts accumulated over processed input symbols. Because $p<1/2$, every unprocessed surviving symbol contributes at most $1-p$. Thus
\begin{equation}
B(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_jp^{m_j(u)}(1-p)^{n-1-m_j(u)}
\end{equation}
is an admissible upper bound on every full candidate extending $u$, where $m_j(u)$ counts observed mismatches already forced under deletion alignment $j$. Best-first expansion with an optional prefix-feasibility oracle is exact when the incumbent score exceeds the largest frontier bound.

The computational package distinguishes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PREFIX-L0}: no code-specific prefix pruning;
\item \textbf{PREFIX-L2}: systematic-linear prefix feasibility, representing code-assisted acceleration rather than membership-only universality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fixed edit-position generalization}
For exactly $t$ uniform deletions, there are $\binom nt$ deletion-position streams. Each stream emits a surviving substitution pattern and $t$ hidden input bits. The same proof yields
\begin{equation}
U_t^{\rm new}=\sum_{S:\,|S|=t}\eta_S.
\end{equation}
The naive number of streams is $\Theta(n^t)$ for fixed $t$. This is polynomial for each fixed $t$ but is an \emph{XP} dependence, not a proof of fixed-parameter tractability $f(t)n^c$. Any FPT claim requires a different merged-state or combinatorial argument.

One insertion has an analogous stream family indexed by insertion position, with the observed inserted symbol probability included in the component mass.

\section{Computational programme}

\subsection{E0: exactness and implementation audit}
The package checks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item one-deletion recurrence, dynamic program, vectorized codeword scoring, and exact rational scoring;
\item one-insertion likelihood against independent vectorized scoring;
\item complete history-driven decoding for one and two deletions;
\item prefix-driven decoding and certificate validity;
\item random-linear, CRC-like, Hamming, and VT membership/codeword construction;
\item zero false certificates in exhaustive small observation spaces.
\end{enumerate}
Any discrepancy produces
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small]
STOP_FIELD_DEFINING_FIBER_PROGRAMME
STAGE=EXACTNESS_OR_CERTIFICATE_FAILURE
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{E1: theory and novelty-boundary audit}
The code verifies three finite identities:
\begin{enumerate}
\item aggregate candidate order equals posterior order under a uniform prior;
\item the random-code conditional success law is
\begin{equation}
\Prb\{\widehat X=X\mid G=g\}
=\frac{\binom{N-g}{M-1}}{\binom{N-1}{M-1}};
\end{equation}
\item the minimal one-deletion multiplicity counterexample is reproduced exactly.
\end{enumerate}
The first two are scientifically useful but not field-defining novelty.

\subsection{E2: path-versus-fiber boundary and search inflation}
For small $n$, enumerate the full ambient input space and compute exact aggregate likelihood ranks. For each random trial, record:
\begin{align}
I_{\rm search}^{\rm opt}&=\frac{Q_{\rm cand}}{G_{\rm posterior}^{\rm optimistic}},\\
I_{\rm search}^{\rm pess}&=\frac{Q_{\rm cand}}{G_{\rm posterior}^{\rm pessimistic}},\\
I_{\rm hist}&=\frac{Q_{\rm hist}}{Q_{\rm cand}}.
\end{align}
The test includes uniform and edge-biased deletion positions and substitution probabilities $p\in\{0,0.05,0.10\}$. It also measures the frequency with which pathwise first-hit returns a non-ML codeword.

\subsection{E3: one deletion plus substitutions}
At $n\in\{12,16,20,24\}$ and rate near $0.75$, compare:
\begin{itemize}
\item HISTORY-L0: membership-only history enumeration with membership-first exact scoring;
\item PREFIX-L0: code-independent prefix bound without prefix pruning;
\item PREFIX-L2: prefix feasibility for systematic linear codes;
\item exhaustive codeword-wise exact ML.
\end{itemize}
Use random-linear and CRC-like codes, $p\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10\}$, and per-block records sufficient to recompute all means and tails.

\subsection{E4--E5: bounded-edit transfer}
E4 tests one insertion plus substitutions at the same lengths and rates. E5 tests two deletions at $n\in\{10,12,14,16,18,20\}$. These are not substitutes for a general transducer theorem; they ask whether the same aggregate-search principle survives two structurally different extensions.

\subsection{E6: code-family transfer}
Use one-deletion plus substitutions on:
\begin{itemize}
\item random systematic linear code;
\item deterministic CRC-like cyclic code;
\item Hamming $(15,11)$ code;
\item nonlinear VT code with a checksum membership oracle.
\end{itemize}
The same channel history engine must remain exact. PREFIX-L2 is reported only where the code exposes the required interface.

\section{Cost accounting and preregistered thresholds}

\subsection{Work vector}
The package does not report ``number of guesses'' alone. It records
\begin{equation}
\mathbf W=(Q_{\rm hist},Q_{\rm cand},Q_{\rm mem},Q_{\rm score},Q_{\rm symbol},Q_{\rm heap},Q_{\rm bound},Q_{\rm prefix},M_{\rm peak},T_{\rm wall}).
\end{equation}
Three scalar work models---optimistic, balanced, and pessimistic---apply frozen weights to these components. The scientific record retains the full vector, so a reviewer can replace the scalarization.

\subsection{Reference baseline}
The initial computational gate uses exhaustive codeword-wise aggregate ML as an independent exact oracle and a first work reference. This is necessary for correctness and for establishing whether candidate-likelihood evaluation is avoided. It is \emph{not} sufficient for a final field-defining claim. The next contract must include exact priority-first/code-trellis methods and specialized synchronization systems.

\subsection{Core thresholds}
The standard profile requires, at $p=0.02$ and $p=0.05$:
\begin{itemize}
\item one deletion: at least two code families, completion at least $0.95$, mean total-work speedup at least $20$, fifth-percentile speedup at least $2$, median candidate-score reduction at least $100$, and favorable work-slope separation;
\item one insertion: at least one family with mean speedup at least $15$;
\item two deletions: at least one family with mean speedup at least $10$;
\item code transfer: at least three families passing at $p=0.05$;
\item boundary: multiple pathwise-error configurations, median search inflation no greater than $20$, and controlled history inflation.
\end{itemize}
These are early-investment thresholds, not claims of universal superiority.

\section{Real use cases and model discipline}
The proposal's strongest application candidates are not generic wireless links but systems in which the receiver truly observes a small, probabilistically weighted alignment uncertainty:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Short-packet timing slips:} missed/duplicated symbol decisions or bounded sample-clock drift, with the synchronizer exporting an alignment lattice rather than a single hard alignment.
\item \textbf{Variable-length demapping:} unequal-length labels or framing errors create insertion/deletion uncertainty; candidate demapping histories are aggregated jointly with FEC membership.
\item \textbf{Storage and DNA:} deletion/insertion/substitution channels and short strands provide natural benchmark domains, though they do not by themselves establish telecommunications relevance.
\item \textbf{Packet-sequence transformations:} bounded loss, duplication, and reordering with outer checksum/network-code membership.
\end{enumerate}
A physical claim requires measured post-front-end statistics. Merely renaming a deletion channel as ``clock slip'' is insufficient.

\section{Scientific interpretation of a positive early result}
A positive standard run would establish four things:
\begin{enumerate}
\item aggregation is operationally necessary because pathwise first-hit can be wrong;
\item the simple certificate can stop far before codebook-wide likelihood evaluation in favorable short, high-rate regimes;
\item membership-first scoring makes the L0 code interface genuinely useful;
\item the same mechanism transfers at least to one insertion, two deletions, and unrelated codebooks.
\end{enumerate}
It would \emph{not} establish:
\begin{itemize}
\item that the method beats the best specialized decoder;
\item an asymptotic complexity exponent advantage;
\item polynomial-time exact decoding for unrestricted transducers;
\item novelty of posterior guesswork;
\item a real telecommunications deployment advantage.
\end{itemize}
The correct outcome in that situation is a revised, sharply de-risked programme rather than either premature celebration or premature termination.

\section{Authorized next contract after a revised pivot}
Only the following four tasks are authorized after a positive computational gate.

\subsection{N1: independent novelty matrix}
For every proposed theorem or algorithmic claim, identify the closest primary source in conditional guesswork, sequential synchronization decoding, priority-first ML decoding, weighted transducers, ambiguous probabilistic automata, and deletion/insertion decoding. Classify each claim as distinct, partially anticipated, known, or unresolved.

\subsection{N2: strong baseline gate}
Implement or obtain reproducible baselines for:
\begin{itemize}
\item priority-first/A$^\star$ decoding on a code trellis;
\item exact channel-code forward or list methods where feasible;
\item VT, Guess-and-Check/GC+, marker/watermark, and synchronization-polar methods in their matched regimes;
\item align-then-decode and best-path approximations;
\item codeword-wise aggregate likelihood and any exact list-pruning alternative.
\end{itemize}
The revised programme stops if its fully accounted advantage disappears.

\subsection{N3: theorem extraction}
Target one of the following, in order of value:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a nontrivial bound on $Q_{\rm cand}/G(X\mid Y)$ or $Q_{\rm hist}/Q_{\rm cand}$ in terms of alignment ambiguity;
\item a strict fixed-parameter/XP boundary for bounded deletions or drift;
\item a hardness theorem showing where exact aggregate candidate search ceases to be favorable;
\item a certified resource-constrained converse under an explicit oracle model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{N4: calibrated use-case gate}
Use one real or high-fidelity front-end model. Freeze the channel law and compare total latency, memory, reliability, and tail behavior. Do not begin FPGA/ASIC work before N1--N3 survive.

The evidence is unusually strong for the one-deletion and one-insertion computational mechanism. It is not yet a field-defining result for four reasons. First, the exact reference is exhaustive codeword-wise likelihood evaluation rather than the full set of strongest specialized baselines. Second, the two-deletion wall-clock gain is modest and based on a deliberately small largest-length sample. Third, the generic information-theoretic interpretation of ``fiber guesswork'' is already conditional posterior guesswork. Fourth, no calibrated physical synchronization trace has been tested.

Accordingly, the original claim set must stop, while a revised and sharply bounded research path is scientifically justified. The next investment gate is N1--N4, not a deep rerun of the same synthetic experiments.

\section{Conclusion}
The attached proposal is scientifically stronger than a superficial ``GRAND for deletions'' description. It identifies the right ML semantic object and supplies a valid exact certificate for a controlled non-invertible channel. Its broad field-defining language nevertheless overstates the novelty of candidate rank and generic bounded search. The kill-fast protocol therefore tests the computational core aggressively while preregistering a mandatory theoretical reframe.

The programme deserves further investment only if exact aggregate search simultaneously demonstrates path-multiplicity relevance, controlled search inflation, strong work reduction, code transfer, and bounded-edit transfer. Even then, the next stage is not hardware or a general transducer library. It is a narrow novelty-and-baseline confrontation followed by extraction of a genuinely new complexity theorem. If those tests fail, the programme must stop immediately.
